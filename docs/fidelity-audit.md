# Fidelity audit

Reviewed 2026-10-21 against game commit `9c963955af732ee932fcf983d3c169d76565a481` and reference remake commit `6ad8c4230a4aa4cd21840f8cb59a38344e41c3e0`.

The current game is a playable settlement strategy prototype using original assets. It is not yet a close reproduction of The Settlers III. Browser stability and passing tests establish that the authored campaign works; they do not establish original-game parity. This audit reads implementation and primary material. It does not claim a side-by-side playthrough of the original executable or a measured fidelity percentage.

## Update: physical economy engine (2026-10-22)

The table below records the 2026-10-21 prototype review and is kept as that record. Since then the browser game runs on `resources/game/engine`, which changes several verdicts:

- **Logistics, workers and construction:** goods live in piles, building slots or a carrier's hands. Deliveries reserve source and destination and change the destination only on arrival. Diggers level real elevation and builders consume delivered material. Workers are individual settlers who fetch a physical tool before taking up a profession. Doorstep backpressure, storage areas, construction-first delivery, the carrier ratio and residences are implemented. A ledger-based oracle checks goods conservation every tick in the engine suites.
- **Production:** woodcutters, foresters, stonecutters, fishermen, farmers, waterworks, millers, bakers, pig farmers, butchers, miners with preferred meals, smelters and smiths run on physical work cycles with finite trees, stone, fish and deposits.
- **Terrain and territory:** the map is a six-neighbour lattice with elevation, slope-aware routing, fog of war and per-player views. Territory comes from military buildings and pioneers. Geologists leave survey signs. Building footprints, protected tiles, doors and pile positions follow the pinned reference remake definitions.
- **Military:** a barracks turns carriers into swordsmen, pikemen and bowmen with weapons delivered to it, at the rank set when they are recruited. Soldiers are individual settlers that garrison towers and castles, march where they are sent, fight in melee or with arrows that fly for a time, and die. A military building whose defenders have all fallen is captured with its goods and land, and buildings left on lost land are destroyed. Health, strength, reach, garrison places and the way available gold scales damage away from home follow reference-remake values; recruitment time and search reach are placeholders (S3-053). Troops are marked and commanded with the manual's controls: rectangles, SHIFT, ALT types, numbered groups, forced moves, waypoints and patrols. Original maps keep their soldiers' classes, ranks and garrisons.
- **Roman specials and religion:** wine-growers plant vines only beside slopes and press ripe grapes into wine; the charcoal maker turns five planks into coal (manual). Wine carried to a small temple becomes manna one offering at a time; manna buys promotions in the reference's rising costs, raising the rank of soldiers recruited afterwards only (manual). A large temple gives manna once and brings priests.
- **Presentation:** carriers render in all six directions, holding the goods they actually carry. Piles, construction stages, saplings and crops are drawn from state.

Still not matched: no value is an admitted original observation. Costs, capacities and preferred meals are manual candidates. Geometry is reference-remake evidence; for barracks the reference definition (5 planks, 4 stone) differs from the manual table (4 planks, 5 stone). Timings, radii and population values are explicit placeholders that name their owning issues. Priests' divine interventions, withering vines, healers, thieves and siege equipment, a computer player that fights, other civilizations, ships, trade, campaigns, original maps in play, the original control center and multiplayer remain open roadmap work.

## Priority for a faithful remake

1. Replace global-stock production with real goods, bounded building inventories, delivery requests, individual carriers and walkable routes. A delivery must change stock only when its carrier arrives. Render those simulation entities instead of decorative commuters.
2. Add genuine worker allocation, tool requirements, building-site delivery and construction work. Establish one source-verified Roman wood-to-plank chain first, including exhausted trees and blocked delivery behavior.
3. Implement finite resource deposits, surveying and terrain/territory rules, then individual military units, garrisons and capture. A rival must participate in the same economy and movement rules.
4. Verify original values and scenario behavior against pinned data and repeatable original-game observations. Add the missing production chains and systems from that evidence, then extend factions and content.
5. Compare original and browser scenes at matched camera positions and states before claiming visual fidelity. Complete directional/action animation and audio integration after the underlying behavior exists.

These are engineering priorities, not claims that those systems are already implemented. More sprites or passing the present campaign tests cannot substitute for the logistics and unit simulation.
