We are fans. We grew up with The Settlers III, and we are not going to pretend to be anything else. We were the kids who placed a woodcutter, then a sawmill, then watched a carrier walk a single plank across the map and felt like we had built a living thing. We lost evenings to it, and some of us lost whole weekends.

This is the first post on the blog for our remake, and it is a promise as much as an update: we want this game to carry on.

## It is not only nostalgia

Nostalgia got us started, but it is not why we are still here. We are still here because the game is **still fun**.

Open almost any modern city builder and you place a building, a number goes up, and you move on. In The Settlers there is no number to cheat. A stonecutter needs a pick before he can work. A builder needs the stone to actually arrive. Nothing arrives unless a carrier carries it, one piece at a time, along a road you laid out. When it goes wrong, you can see why: a doorstep piled high with logs nobody collects, a sawmill idling because the forester planted too few trees. When it goes right, a whole settlement hums, and you did that.

That loop of build, notice, fix and watch it breathe is as good now as it was when we first played it. It deserves to be playable by someone who has never heard of it, on whatever they have in their pocket, without hunting for an old CD or a compatibility patch.

## What you can play today

The game runs in your browser, and you can try a small settlement right on the home page without an account.

- **A real economy.** Every settler, tool and good is a real object. Carriers fetch each plank and stone, diggers level the site, builders build from what was delivered, and a workplace without its tool tells you so instead of hiring a phantom worker. There are 32 Roman building types and 26 goods in the simulation.
- **The original tutorial map.** Decoded from the free Amazons demo, with its own terrain, tower and starting goods.
- **A computer rival.** It surveys, expands, builds an economy and attacks using the same commands you do. Capture every enemy castle and tower to win.
- **Online duels.** Host a match, share a five-letter code and face a friend on one landscape. The server runs the match and both screens stay in step.
- **Keyboard first.** Choose a building and the cursor jumps to the nearest valid site. Press Enter to place it.

## Where it is honestly unfinished

This is a work in progress, and we would rather say so plainly than oversell it.

- Only the Roman settlement is playable. The Egyptians, the Asians and the Amazons are not there yet.
- The original campaigns and missions are not played yet.
- Ships, harbours, colonies and trade are not built.
- Promotions, the religious interventions and large parts of warfare are partial.
- The look and the sound are close to the original in places and not close enough in others. We keep an audit that lists what matches and what does not, and a feature only counts as done when it passes in a real browser.
- Online duels are two players on a generated map. There is no fog-of-war protection against a modified client, no spectators and no computer fill-in yet.

If you spot something that does not feel like the game you remember, that is exactly the bug report we want.

## What comes next

We are working through it in the open: more than a hundred tracked issues across a dozen themes, from the rest of the economy and the other civilizations, to campaigns, ships and trade, to a native app so it does not have to live in a tab. Each claim of fidelity is tied to evidence rather than to our memory of how it felt.
